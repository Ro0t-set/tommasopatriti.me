Experimental design

The first series starts with the PPO checkpoint from the project by Manuel Andruccioli, Tommaso Patriti and Giacomo Totaro. The second builds a geometric actor from scratch. Its coefficients come from formulas using LiDAR rays, the car footprint and the vehicle dynamics. Simulation feedback guides the selection and revision of formulas. The resulting policy receives only LiDAR at inference. Original project report · Methodological reference

Controllers share the track, starting pose and noise seed for each comparison. Success requires at least 99% of ordered waypoints and a start-line crossing without a collision. The time limit is 200 simulated seconds. The final verification freezes checkpoint hashes and uses five fresh seeds on each of 21 tracks. Two earlier verification rounds rejected candidates after collisions at Austin. A fourth controller in the last comparison isolates the speed edit. Full comparisons

Reported lap time change is the mean of 100 × (candidate time / reference time − 1) over the same completed starts. Collisions and timeouts stay in the completion denominator. A separate audit checks the full 58 × 31 cm rectangle against occupied pixels at each recorded pre-action pose. Map resolution, 0.01 s sampling and the tyre model limit conclusions about real-world contact and controlled drift. Trajectory audit
